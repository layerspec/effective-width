## III. What sets the width of a layer: one object, one principle, one chain run in two directions

**(Reorganised 2026-10-07 after the author's account of the original motivation: PCA's sequential, orthogonal extraction — each component the best given the previous ones, the fewest components for the most information — carried into a network layer by layer. The subsections III-A–E below are unchanged in content; this preamble states the single principle they share and the single chain they form, and names the one place where the PCA picture and the trained network pull in opposite directions.)**

**The object.** Throughout we measure one quantity: the channel covariance Σ_l of the output of convolution l, sampled over images and positions, summarised by k*(τ), the number of principal directions holding a fraction τ of the variance, divided by the reachable rank r_max of the layer.

**The principle.** For a positive semidefinite Σ, max{tr(PΣ) : P an orthogonal projection of rank k} = λ₁ + … + λ_k, attained by the leading eigenvectors (Ky Fan's maximum principle); k*(τ) is therefore the smallest k at which the best k-dimensional linear extraction keeps τ of the variance — the fewest orthogonal components for a prescribed share of the information, which is the PCA optimum. Every statement in this paper that bounds a width is this principle applied to a particular covariance, and should be read as one proposition with several instances rather than as separate results:
- applied to Σ_l itself, the truncation error of the fold is exactly the discarded tail, Σ_{i>k} λ_i (Prop. S7 `prop:truncation`), and the oblique fold into the next layer inherits the same bound (`prop:shrink`); across layers, the convexity of the tail in k makes the water-filling allocation optimal (`prop:fill`);
- applied to a sample covariance, the convexity of the Ky Fan functional (a supremum of linear functionals) gives the direction of the finite-sample bias without a spectral gap (Th2, proofs note) — the n/C gate of §II is this instance;
- applied to the covariance of the (class, position) cell means, it gives the terminal cap k*_τ ≤ P·K − 1 once the variance outside their span is below 1 − τ (Lemma B9.0, III-C);
- applied to the output-gradient Gram Γ instead of Σ_l, the same count is the "number of active neurons" of Cui et al. (SIAM J. Math. Data Sci. 2020, Definition 3.1, with its Poincaré error bound), which we measure at channel level in III-C (B7) — the task-side width;
- the two covariances the principle is applied to, Σ_patch and Γ, are the two Kronecker factors of the K-FAC approximation to the layer's Fisher matrix (Grosse and Martens 2016), so the forward and backward widths of this section are the τ-widths of the two factors of the curvature, and the transfer matrix of B10 is what sits between them.
What the principle does not give is the eigenvalues themselves: where Σ_l comes from is the chain.

**The chain, forward at initialisation.** Three kinds of step occur. (1) A convolution: conditional on its input patches, the output covariance is in law W̃ Σ_patch W̃ᵀ with W̃ standard Gaussian (`prop:law`; exact at finite width, and the law depends on the data only through the spectrum of Σ_patch). (2) A residual addition: conditional on the joint covariance J of the stream and the last convolution's input patches, Σ_y = [I W] J [I W]ᵀ is again an exact sampling law (Proposition B12, proofs note), but one that depends on J as a matrix — the cross block between stream and branch input cannot be dropped, and the free additive convolution of the two spectra is *not* its large-width limit [§10.48–10.50]. (3) An activation: no exact law; the Gaussian closed form fails at initialisation (the stream is non-centred) and the step is reported as measured (≤ 2 channels in bottleneck blocks, −10 to −20 % in basic blocks) [§10.37–10.39, §10.47]. The patch covariance feeding the next convolution is a fixed linear image of the post-activation covariance (`prop:depth`, `prop:compression`), so the sequence Σ₁ → Σ₂ → … is Markov in the activation law with the first-layer patch Gram as its only input from the data (`prop:gram`) — the "dependence of the next component on the ones already extracted" of the PCA picture, now across depth — and it can be run numerically from the stem (the A7 surrogate, III-A). What no link supplies is a closed form for the width. III-A states for each step how far it is a theorem and verifies the whole on six families (order ρ 0.95–1.00, level within 0.05).

**The chain, backward after training.** After training the same object is no longer set by the input side. The trained weight is a non-negative combination of the batch gradients of a known memory window (Lemma B7.1), so the column space of Σ_out lies in the span of the position-resolved output gradients of that window: Σ_out ⊆ span(Γ_win), Σ_out = Γ^{1/2} M̃ Γ^{1/2} on that span (B10), and Γ itself is built from the ReLU masks and the per-image error norms (EL2N) of the window. The chain now runs from the loss backward: the gradient Gram sets the set of admissible directions, the Σ_patch-coherence of their input filters (M̃) sets how many of them the output uses, and where collapse is complete the cell means set the cap (B9.0). III-C states which of these links are exact (B6, B7.1, B10's identity, B9.0) and which are measured only (the coherence of M̃, the hard-example count, the moment w_out crosses 1 − τ — together, κ).

**Where the PCA picture and the trained network diverge.** The PCA optimum is a *reconstruction* optimum: keep the most variance in the fewest orthogonal directions. A classifier's optimum in its late layers is the opposite: pull same-class samples together, which lowers the variance width (neural collapse). The data show the second, not the first. The late τ-width *falls* with optimisation steps at fixed data (d = 0.38 against 0.52 at equal steps [A25]; both the numerical rank and k*(0.999) contract from 0.49 → 0.18 and 0.92 → 0.55 of C between 5,000 and 39,000 steps [A34]), while the gradient Gram stays at least 3K wide in every layer (63/63 [P-b7.2]); and forcing the kernels to be exactly orthogonal (ONI, T = 20) raises the width from 0.35–0.36 to 0.68–0.73 r_max in two architectures and lowers accuracy by 0.10–0.16 points [D5, `checkpoint_analysis.txt` §13]. So three widths must be kept apart: the width a PCA-optimal encoder would use (the orthogonalised network), the width the trained network *does* use (the ruler, Σ_out), and the width the task *needs* (the gradient side, Γ, and the cell cap). The ruler reads the second; it is neither of the other two, which is why "variance kept" and "task importance" disagree (as also seen in weighted-SVD compression of transformers, Hsu et al. 2022) and why III-C has to go through the gradient at all. The reconstruction picture survives in one place — the fold is loss-free exactly where the trained width is already below the nominal one — and the sequential-dependence picture survives in both chains; the orthogonality picture does not survive training.

The literature on deep representations contains, spread over five lines of work, everything needed to say what determines Σ_l at initialisation, how training moves it, and what bounds it at the end. No one of those lines states the layer-wise result; we assemble it here, one link per subsection, and state for each link the hypothesis under which it is a theorem and the measured degree to which that hypothesis holds for convolutional networks.

### III-A. Initialisation: the spectrum is a function of architecture, initialisation scale and the image second-order statistics

**Theorem-form summary (added 2026-10-11; proofs §M; numbers §10.53–10.59; wording rules pending I4, I7–I9).** Fix a layer's realised input patches (positions × images; dependent, non-centred, no assumption on them) and draw its kernel with i.i.d. Gaussian entries. (a) *Determinacy, exact at finite width and finite sample.* The joint law of the convolution-step and activation-step covariances depends on the data only through the Gram matrix of the patches, i.e. on the empirical patch measure up to rotation (Prop. S7 gives this for the whole network). For the convolution step this reduces to the spectrum of Σ_patch (Prop. S6, to be stated as the channel-side form of Novak et al. 2019 Eq. 3–6 / Xiao et al. 2018 Lemma A.1 / Aitchison 2020 Eq. 16–17). For the activation step it reduces to the spectrum of Σ_patch together with the coordinates of μ_patch in its eigenbasis *only under the Gaussian surrogate for the patch measure* (T1.2; the surrogate is exact for the first layer on Gaussian inputs, 8 = 8, and over-predicts the measured width by 0–0.14 r_max elsewhere, §10.53); under the surrogate, given the spectrum of the convolution-step covariance and the coordinates of its mean, the law is moreover an explicit Haar-rotation law (T2a; compatible with 11/13 VGG layers, not with the residual stream, whose law needs the joint covariance J, Prop. B12). (b) *Concentration, exact and non-asymptotic.* The τ-width is a random variable of the draw. Its tail sum concentrates about its Monte Carlo mean with a Lipschitz constant that depends on the data only through the top eigenvalue of the patch second moment (uncentred for the activation step, which lets the stream's DC component in), with scale 1/√C (Theorem M.2 and Lemma M.2.0, which carry Th5/Th5′ through the ReLU and the sample centring; Gaussian concentration and the Poincaré inequality; the "width is the sample size" reading of Koltchinskii and Lounici 2017). On the stored input spectra of 515 dense convolutions of ten random families, the Chebyshev interval guaranteed at probability 0.80 contains the measured width in 515/515 layers at both thresholds; at τ = 0.95 its half-width has median 0.057 r_max (8 channels), it reaches r_max in 1 % of the layers (all C ≤ 24), it is six times the Monte Carlo SD of k*, and the measured width sits a median 0.8 channels from the Monte Carlo mean [§10.59, P9.126–127]. At τ = 0.999 the half-width is 0.022 r_max but the interval reaches r_max in 26 % of the layers (C ≤ 96) [exploratory]. The activation step is not checked (patches are not stored). (c) *Computability.* Two levels of sampling, polynomial cost, no images needed, no closed form at either level (M.3; this is the A7 surrogate). (d) *Interval.* Under the surrogate the activation-step width lies in [k_lo2, k_hi], both computable from the convolution-step covariance and mean (M.4; Lemma T2.1 is Bapat and Sunder 1985): the lower end is the linear part, 0.67 of the surrogate width in median, and the upper end is tight only for the C = 1024 spiked streams [§10.54]. The corollary chain — the r_max bound, Weyl and Ky Fan at the addition (B11.0, B12), no narrowing of the zero-mean ReLU step and narrowing only through the mean weighting diag Φ(m_c/s_c) (T2.2′), the centring shift τ″ = τ − (1−τ)β (T1.3), the Σ_patch-angle 1/√r_eff that turns the literature's near-orthogonality assumption into a measurable quantity (T1.4), truncation and spike peeling (T2a.1–2) — is tabulated with the measured gap of each link in proofs §M.5. *What the theorem does not do:* give the position of the mean curve (simulated), cover the real non-Gaussian patch measure at the activation step, cover the residual stream, or say anything about training.


**Known.** For i.i.d. Gaussian weights the pre-activation of layer l+1 at a set of positions is, conditional on layer l, Gaussian with a
position covariance obtained by convolving the post-activation covariance of layer l with the kernel's spatial support
(Xiao et al. 2018, eq. (2.7), Σ^{l+1} = A ⋆ C(Σ^l), Lemma A.1 for c → ∞; the fully connected case is Poole et al. 2016 and Schoenholz et al. 2017). The map from Σ_l to Σ_{l+1}
therefore factorises into a *linear* step, whose spectrum is given exactly at finite width by the Silverstein (1995) law for
W̃ Σ W̃ᵀ with W̃ Gaussian (our Proposition S6), and a *non-linear* step (the activation), for which the mean-field literature gives the
infinite-width covariance map (the C-map of Poole et al.) and the linear-width literature gives a random-matrix law under
independence assumptions (Fan and Wang 2020; Louart, Liao and Couillet 2018). With batch normalisation the recursion has a
stable fixed point at which the batch Gram is isometric (Yang et al. 2019, BSB1 fixed point; Joudaki, Daneshmand and Bach 2023,
Theorem 4: the distance to isometry decays as exp(−ℓ log β_σ) with β_ReLU = 2 − 2/π), and its finite-width deviation is bounded by
exp(−α·depth) + n/√d (Joudaki, Daneshmand and Bach, ICML 2023, Theorem 1, under a geometric-ergodicity assumption the authors
leave open). The scale of the initialisation enters the same recursion (γ of the last BN in a block, LayerScale in transformers;
Kunin et al. 2021 for the dynamics it induces).

**Hypotheses and how far they hold.** (i) *Infinite channels.* We measure at C = 64–2048; the layer-local law (Prop. S6) is exact at
finite C for the linear step, and on 535 dense convolutions of 11 families its median deviation from the measured k* is ≤ 2 channels (≤ 0.016 r_max) at both thresholds, 94–100 % of the layers of each family within 0.05 r_max, rank correlation ≥ 0.974 [supp. Tables S18–S19]. (ii) *Gaussian pre-activations across positions.* Violated: the marginals have
excess kurtosis, and the copula diagnostic shows that at τ = 0.999 the ReLU step is determined by the Gaussian copula together with
the *full* per-channel marginals (13/13 ReLUs of VGG-16 within 0.03), but not by marginals matched to four cumulants (misses by 0.03–0.08), and at τ = 0.95 the deep layers need the pairwise dependence as well; the closed forms (arc-cosine recursion, Gaussian equivalence, linear term) over- or under-shoot by 0.05–0.2 [supp. law paragraph; feasibility note C.1–C.3]. (iii) *Independent samples.* The theorems of Fan–Wang, Louart et al. and both Joudaki papers
take the n samples independent; ours are positions of the same image, and neighbouring patches are the "duplicated samples" that
Joudaki et al. 2023 explicitly exclude. This is the hypothesis that fails most, and it is why the predicted *widening* with depth of a
normalised network (Gram → identity) is not what the position-sampled covariance shows [exploratory, seed 1: ρ(depth, k*₀.₉₅/r_max)
between −0.3 and +0.8 across ten families, none monotone to full width]. (iv) *Freeness at the residual addition.* The natural hypothesis that the branch's covariance sits in generic (Haar) position relative to the stream's, which would make the addition a free additive convolution of two spectra, is false and does not become true as C grows: the measured sum is narrower than the Haar superposition in 56/56 blocks of four random ResNets (by 0.06–0.35), the gap grows with stage depth in ResNet-34/50, and it sits in the stream–branch cross covariance, which carries 12–26 % of the energy along the sum's top directions while having trace ≈ 0 [§10.48, P9.105–107]. The exact statement that replaces freeness is Proposition B12: given J = Cov([x; p]) of the stream x and the last convolution's input patches p, Σ_y = [I W]J[I W]ᵀ has a law fixed by J and the scale of W; sampling W reproduces the measured width within 5 % (bottleneck, 13/13 blocks of layer2–4) and 10 % (basic, 23/24 blocks of layer1–4), with draw-to-draw spread of 0.5–3.5 channels, while the same draws without the cross block are off by 5–51 % [§10.49–10.50, P9.108–109].

**What we verify.** (1) The profile at initialisation is computable from the architecture and the image covariance alone: on six
families the surrogate built from the image spectrum alone reproduces the measured profile in order (ρ 0.95–1.00, at or above the random–random ceiling) and level (median difference +0.046) [A7, feasibility note; seed 1].
(2) The image statistics enter only through the first-layer patch Gram (Prop. S7), and they matter: replacing the ImageNet spectrum
by the CIFAR-10 spectrum at the same resolution lowers the level by 0.06–0.24 and, in VGG-16 and ResNet-50, the order to ρ 0.67/0.74 against ceilings of 0.79/0.94 [A7, P-a1 6/6]. (3) *Exploratory (three seeds; P9.85 holds, P9.86 fails, plan §10.25):* the effect of the
image spectrum decays with depth in the plain, dense and inverted-residual families (relative difference in the last third 0.01–0.26 at τ = 0.95,
≤ 0.01 at τ = 0.999, 9/9 runs) — the "input forgetting" of Joudaki et al. It does not decay in ResNet-18 (0.52–0.60 in the last third)
and does decay in ResNet-50 (0.16–0.21): the slowing by residual connections predicted by Joudaki and Hofmann (2024, Prop. 2:
κ_res = (1−r²)κ + r²ρ) is seen in one of the two residual families, so it is reported, not claimed as a property of identity shortcuts. (4) The initialisation scale is an input, not a nuisance: zero-initialising
the last BN of each block (timm) versus γ = 1 (torchvision) changes the random reference level from 0.29–0.34 to 0.44–0.48 in
ResNet-18 and the random–random agreement from 0.98 to 0.71 [§10.16]; and the trained-vs-random rank correlation is a property of the *pair* (trained profile, draw), not of the trained network: against γ = 1 and γ = 0 draws ResNet-50 reads −0.08 and +0.44, ResNet-34 +0.13 and +0.61, ResNet-101 −0.59 and +0.20, with ResNet-18 unchanged at +0.73 (Table families, columns *draw* and *other draw*; A33, §10.22). The order statistic that needs no reference is the within-stage trend: at γ = 1 the stream's width falls along a stage (the addition narrows it, B12); at γ = 0 the stage is flat because every branch is zero [§10.36]. (5) The within-stage fall at γ = 1 is reproduced by the surrogate on the matched spectrum and on white noise alike (ρ −0.83…−0.94) — an architectural property — and a stream that enters narrower than its branches rises instead (CIFAR images at 224 px; ResNet-18's basic blocks) [§10.40–10.41, P9.97–99].

*Conclusion of the link.* The order of the initial profile is an architectural quantity once the draw's initialisation scale is fixed; its level is set jointly by the architecture,
the initialisation scale and the image spectrum. The theory that predicts it is an exact conditional sampling law at every convolution (S6) and at every residual addition (B12, where the free-convolution hypothesis fails and the joint covariance is needed), and only measured at the activation step, where the failing hypotheses are Gaussianity and sample independence. No step has a closed form for the width; the profile is simulated, not derived.

### III-B. Early training: alignment precedes the width change

**Known.** In deep linear networks the input–output singular modes are learned in order of singular value (Saxe et al. 2014); with
small initialisation the kernel's eigenstructure aligns with the task before the loss falls ("silent alignment", Atanasov et al. 2022);
one large gradient step adds a rank-one spike aligned with the target to an otherwise unchanged bulk (Ba et al. 2022), and at small
learning rate the bulk of the conjugate kernel is invariant (Wang et al. 2023, Lemma 5.1/Theorem 5.2). For every layer of a trained
CNN the Gram of the kernel, WᵀW, is proportional to the gradient outer product of its input patches (the convolutional neural feature
ansatz, Beaglehole et al. 2023; Radhakrishnan et al. 2024), and at convergence with weight decay this proportionality is a first-order
condition (Boix-Adserà et al. 2025). The first layer aligns with the data principal components (Maennel et al. 2020). In transformers the
rank of W_t − W_0 grows gradually (Boix-Adserà et al. 2023).

**Hypotheses and how far they hold.** Small initialisation and whitened data (Atanasov), two layers or linear width (Ba, Wang),
convergence with weight decay (Boix-Adserà 2025) — none holds for a standard-initialised, batch-normalised CNN under SGD; and in a
conv–BN layer the first-order condition of FACT cannot hold at all (Prop. B6 below).

**What we verify.** The gain of each kernel aligns with the principal directions of its input covariance (ρ_align ≈ +0.9) and does so by
epoch 7–15 in 9/9 runs, before the profile settles (epoch 15–100) [§10.7: P9.11 registered sign fails, reverse holds 7/9; 19 ImageNet weights median +0.91, six ResNet-50 recipes +0.86 to +0.95; inverted-residual families +0.11 to +0.61]; the numerical rank of the late layers rises
from 0.11 to 0.72 of C within the first 25 steps [check: A34 part b]. The alignment is only partly a shadow of the neural feature ansatz:
the gain follows the output-side gradient energy first (Spearman +0.98/+0.95) and, holding that fixed, the input variance second
(+0.55/+0.45) [A32, §10.21]. The counterfactual holds: forcing exact isometry of the kernel (ONI, T = 20) moves the profile by the
amount the decomposition predicts (|out − pred| 0.021–0.031, realised fraction 0.91–1.04, two architectures, three seeds) [D5].

### III-C. Late training: what caps the late layers, and where the cap can be measured

**Known.** Every collapse theorem puts the late-layer rank at or below K. With weight decay, every stationary point of the regularised loss satisfies λW_l = −∇_{W_l}L₀ (eq. (4) of Zangrando et al.), and if the class means of the features entering layer l have small total within-class variation TCV_K, the distance of W_l to the rank-≤K matrices is at most M_{l,j}·TCV_{K,j}/λ (Zangrando et al., TMLR 2026, Theorem 3.5). In the deep unconstrained-features model the global optimum is a strongly-regular-graph solution whose intermediate features and weights have rank Θ(√K) before the ReLU and K after it (Súkeník, Lampert and Mondelli 2024, Theorem 5 and Definition 4; K ≥ 6 with L ≥ 4, or K ≥ 10 with L = 3), of rank at most q_K ≤ 2 at large depth (Garrod and Keating 2025, Theorems 2–3), with deep neural collapse a positive-semidefinite critical point whose share of the landscape grows with width (ibid., Theorem 5); for deep regularised ResNets and transformers the end-to-end problem reduces, as L → ∞ with λ independent of L, to a generalised UFM whose optima collapse in the *last* layer (Súkeník, Lampert and Mondelli 2025, Theorem 4.3 and Corollary 4.5). Depth alone biases toward low rank: at any global minimum of the L2-regularised loss some hidden representation has s₂/s₁ = O(L^{−1/4}) (Jacot 2023, Proposition 4 and Theorem 1; fully connected, homogeneous activations), and collapse happens in the terminal phase (Papyan, Han and Donoho 2020). The intermediate-layer statements are for fully connected surrogate layers; the only result for residual architectures speaks of the last layer.

**What we measure is above K by a factor 2–50, and the collapsing object is a different one.** The late-layer τ-width on CIFAR-10 is 20–50 channels for K = 10, the numerical rank (eigenvalues above 10⁻³λ₁) 68–138 channels [A34], and a ten-class ImageNet ResNet-18 reads 0.95 of its 512 channels [A26]. On 21 CIFAR checkpoints (VGG-16_BN; K = 10/13/20/50/100; three seeds each) the within-class variance fraction of the *pooled* post-ReLU features falls from 0.86 to 0.07 at the last convolution on CIFAR-10 (0.41 at K = 100), while the rank-K tail energy of the last three weights stays at 0.41–0.62 and the position-sampled τ-width stays at tens of channels [A35, plan §10.24]: NC1 is reached in the pooled representation, and neither the weights nor the position-sampled covariance collapse. Both rulers on the same network (numerical rank and k*(0.999)) contract with optimisation steps and neither reaches K [A34]. This is consistent with the hypothesis of the wide-network theorem (Jacot, Súkeník, Wang and Mondelli 2024: the collapsing object is the input of the final linear layers), and the optimiser decides whether collapse occurs at all (Zhao, Cheng, Masarczyk and Lucchi, ICLR 2026: SGD and Adam reach it, AdamW does not; every CIFAR run here is SGD with momentum and coupled decay).

**The backward chain, in three links.** The remainder of this subsection is the chain of the preamble run from the loss backward: (1) the rank-K stationary-point bound does not apply to a conv–BN layer (B6); (2) the trained weight is a memory window of gradients, so the output covariance lives inside the window's gradient Gram and the transfer matrix M̃ locates the narrowing (B7, B10) — the Ky Fan count on Γ here is Cui et al.'s active-neuron count read at channel level; (3) where collapse is complete, the Ky Fan count on the cell means gives the cap P·K − 1 (B9.0). Each link is stated with what is exact and what is measured.

**Proposition B6 (the rank-K bound is stated for a setting that excludes the layers we measure).** Theorem 3.5 of Zangrando et al. is proved for fully connected feedforward networks without normalisation layers (their §2: "a fully connected L-layer feedforward neural network"); the proposition below shows that its stationarity equation has no non-zero solution in a conv–BN layer, so the theorem does not transfer to the object of this paper rather than being refuted by it. Let layer l be a convolution (with or without bias) followed by batch normalisation in training mode with ε = 0. Then L₀(cW_l) = L₀(W_l) for all c > 0 — the per-channel batch mean removes the bias exactly, the batch standard deviation removes the scale — hence ⟨∇_{W_l}L₀, W_l⟩ = 0, and the only solution of λW_l = −∇_{W_l}L₀ with λ > 0 is W_l = 0. For ε > 0, ⟨∇_{W_l}L₀, W_l⟩ = Σ_c [ε/(σ_c² + ε)] ⟨∂L₀/∂y_c, y_c − β_c⟩ = O(ε/σ²_min). *Proof.* Differentiate L₀(cW_l) at c = 1 (for ε > 0, dy_c/dc = (y_c − β_c) ε/(σ_c² + ε)) and take the inner product of the stationarity equation with W_l. ∎ *Corollary B6′.* A stationary *direction* of the weight, (∇L₀ + λW) ∥ W, requires ∇L₀ = 0. The scale invariance is classical (van Laarhoven 2017; Li and Arora 2020, Lemma 1.3; Zhang, Wang and Xu 2019; Kunin et al. 2021), and the zero cosine was observed at initialisation by Chen, Bu and Yan (2024: |cos| ≤ 3·10⁻⁴ over all channels of VGG16_bn) and attributed to high dimension; B6 says it is exact and holds along the whole trajectory, so the stationarity hypothesis of Theorem 3.5 cannot be met by any conv–BN layer (the theorem itself is stated for networks without normalisation). Measured: the training-mode cosine is |cos| ≤ 0.043 in all 21 × 13 layers (≤ 0.003 outside the 39,000-step arm) and the stationarity residual ‖λW + ∇L₀‖/‖λW‖ is 1.6–13 — no layer is near eq. (4) [A35]. What weight decay sets in such a layer is the norm, not the rank: under SGD with decay the squared norm of a scale-invariant layer converges to √(Lη/(2λ)) and the angular update to √(2ηλ) (Wan et al. 2021, Theorem 1; Li and Arora 2020). Garrod, Keating and Thrampoulidis (2026) show that depth alone yields collapse in the deep UFM without L2; B6 removes the quantitative bound, not collapse itself.

**Lemma B7.1 (the trained weight is a memory window of gradients; the window is known).** The EMA view of weight decay (Wang and Aitchison, ICML 2025, §3.1; Everett and Qiu 2026, §3.1) puts the trained weight in the span of the updates of the last ≈ 1/(ηλ) steps. For SGD with momentum μ_t (Nesterov or classical), decay λ and any schedule η_t this is exact: W_T = a_T W_0 − Σ_{s<T} c_{T,s} g_s, with g_s the batch gradient of the unregularised loss and (a_T, c_{T,s}) the impulse response of the scalar recursion; if η̄λ[1 + μ̄ + μ̄² + 2μ̄³/(1−μ̄)] ≤ (1−μ̄)/2 then every c_{T,s} ≥ 0 and 0 < a_T < 1 (proofs note B7.1, B7.1′; verified to 10⁻¹⁰ against the optimiser). For our recipe a_T = 2·10⁻⁴, 90 % of Σ c² lies in epochs 65–86, and the training loss reaches its terminal phase only after epoch 80–85: the trained weight is built from pre-collapse gradients, and the residual-initialisation alternative is excluded (⟨W_T, W_0⟩/‖W_0‖² = 0.003–0.05 in layers 2–13). Since the gradient of a convolution is Σ δ xᵀ, col(W_T − a_T W_0) ⊆ span{δ}, the position-resolved output gradients over the window — a statement with content for convolutions, where Galanti et al.'s (2024, Lemma 3.2) count of rank-one terms (patches × batch × window) exceeds C. At the last convolution δ_{i,p} = (1/HW) J_BNᵀ D_{i,p} W_cᵀ e_i with e_i = softmax_i − onehot_i and D_{i,p} the ReLU mask of the position: without masks the span is at most K-dimensional and the collapse bound reappears; with masks it is full, and its weight is set by ‖e_i‖, the EL2N score of Paul et al. (2021).

*Measured.* Over the last three convolutions of the 21 checkpoints, k*(0.999) of the output covariance follows k*(0.999) of the output-gradient Gram Γ (Spearman +0.86; +0.93 at τ = 0.95); Γ is at least 3K wide in every layer and narrows with optimisation steps (median 310 → 108 between 5,000 and 39,000 steps) [P-b7.2, P-b7.3]. The forward prediction from the ReLU masks and the EL2N scores reproduces the *order* of Γ's head exactly (ρ = 1.000 in 21/21) but over-counts it by 1.6–2.1× on the fully trained CIFAR-10 networks; the Gram width follows the fraction of images with EL2N > 0.1 (Spearman +0.85) but is not separated from K in this design (partial +0.58) [P-b7.5, P-b7.6]. With the exact window weights c_s on the epoch-40–100 checkpoints, the window Gram orders the final output widths (Spearman +1.00 over the wide layers of three ruler-width runs, +0.83–0.88 after division by the channel count) and the inclusion k*(Σ_out) ≤ k*(Γ_win) holds in every layer of every run [P-b7.1′, P-b7.4b]. In a decay sweep (λ = 5e-5, 5e-4, 1e-3, 2e-3, 5e-3; two seeds) the window's half-mass epoch moves from 50 to 76 to 82–90, the output width is nearer the end-point Gram at the largest λ and nearer the run average at the smallest [P-b7.4c], and among arms of equal accuracy the late level falls with λ — 0.42 → 0.078 → 0.076 → 0.070 of C at 5e-5 / 5e-4 / 1e-3 / 2e-3, an order of magnitude between the first two and nearly flat thereafter [P-b7.4d′, plan §10.32]. *As a bound the inclusion is loose:* on the full-width network the late window Gram is 475 wide against 40 for the output covariance; it is tight only where the layer already sits at its ruler width. B7 is an inclusion and an ordering; the weight decay chooses the window the width is read from.

**The transfer matrix: where the narrowing happens [B10, plan §9.31, §10.33].** Write Γ_win = U diag(γ) Uᵀ, keep the head H = k*(0.999) of its directions, and set M̃ = diag(γ)^{−1/2} Uᵀ Σ_out U diag(γ)^{−1/2}, so that Σ_out = U diag(γ)^{1/2} M̃ diag(γ)^{1/2} Uᵀ on range(U). In the window model (W = Δ̃X̃ᵀ, Δ̃ = U diag(γ)^{1/2} Vᵀ) one has M̃ = VᵀMV with M = X̃ᵀΣ_patch X̃, so M̃_ij = ⟨X̃v_i, X̃v_j⟩_{Σ_patch} is the Σ_patch-inner product of the input filters attached to gradient directions i and j; the identity itself is trivial. Measured on the nine full- and ruler-width VGG-16 runs and the four new decay arms: (i) the diagonal of M̃ has no trend in γ (log–log slope −0.16 to +0.14 — this is the exponent-one observation along the window Gram, 0.91–1.08 over nine runs, 0.94–1.01 on the four unseen at registration [P-b8.5]), and keeping only the diagonal reproduces Γ's width in 16/18 late layers [P-b10.1]: the energies of the gradient directions narrow nothing. (ii) In collapsed layers M̃ is an order of magnitude narrower than Γ (k*(0.999) 32–112 against H 121–485 over eight runs) and 1.3–2.8× wider than Σ_out (24–40): most of the narrowing from hundreds of gradient directions to a few tens of output directions is the Σ_patch-coherence of their input filters, the remainder the interaction of that coherence with the γ-weights [P-b10.2 fails its registered threshold only on the uncollapsed λ = 5e-5 layers, where there is no narrowing to locate]. (iii) Coherence grows with the decay: median k*(M̃)/H over the late layers 0.62 → 0.15 → 0.12 at λ = 5e-5, 5e-4, 2e-3, both seeds [P-b10.4]. (iv) In the stem rank M̃ ≤ rank Σ_patch = 27 whatever Γ does (measured 4–13 against H 5–60) — the r_max bound of §II read from the input side. The nearest hypothesis in the literature is the canonical representation hypothesis of Ziyin et al. (2025), Σ_out ∝ WWᵀ ∝ Γ, which in this notation says M̃ ≈ cI; in position-resolved conv–BN layers the two matrices share neither basis (co-diagonal fraction 0.17–0.23 against 0.09–0.15 for a random basis) nor exponent (0.12–0.76 against the predicted 2) [P-b8.1–8.4], and their theorem is for fully connected layers at a local minimum, which B6 removes. κ is thereby located in a measurable object; why M̃ becomes coherent during training is not derived.

**Where collapse is complete, the width is positions × classes [B9, plan §9.29, §10.28, §10.31].** *Lemma B9.0.* Let a layer's position-sampled output have P positions and K classes, and let w_out be the fraction of its variance outside the span of the P·K (class, position) cell means. If w_out ≤ 1 − τ then k*_τ ≤ P·K − 1 (total-variance decomposition; Ky Fan). *Measured, predictions committed before the spectra were opened:* in the two trained ten-class VGG-16 CIFAR arms the largest eigenvalue gap of the three last-block layers (P = 2×2) sits at 39 or 40 in 18/18 layers (ratio 10–77) and k*(0.999) is 39 or 40; blind, at K = 13 and 20 the gap is at 51/52 and 79 in the 9/9 layers that have one. The cell means are linearly independent (rank 38–39 of 39, 18/18); grouping by class alone leaves 79–95 % of the variance, by class and position 2–25 % (15/18); that remainder lies inside the span of the cell means (w_out 0.0006–0.0012, on either side of 1 − τ, which is why the width is 39 in some layers and 40 in others). The cell structure is present after 5,000 steps while the width is still 110–386: the cap is set early, the width reaches it only when w_out falls below 1 − τ. *Scope.* This holds in VGG-16 on CIFAR-10 at 32 px only. With the input upsampled to 48 px (P = 9, cap 90) the same recipe shows no gap after 100 epochs and widths of 95–213, with 0.003–0.023 of the variance outside the span — above 1 − τ, so the premise fails and with it the cap [P-b9.10/9.11, two seeds, blind]; at K = 50 and 100 there is no gap; the ten-class ImageNet ResNet-18 exceeds 49·10 in 11 of 12 last-stage sites [P-b9.8]. B9 names the width where collapse is complete; it does not say when collapse is complete.

**What enters κ, measured.** The width kept by the ruler rises monotonically with K (0.88/0.92/0.98/0.99 at K = 13/20/50/100, 3/3 seeds) [A24] and contracts with optimisation steps at fixed data (d = 0.38 against 0.52 at equal steps) [A25]; a ten-class ImageNet task at 224 px leaves no idle width in a residual network [A26] and none in a plain one beyond 0.52–0.62 of the parameters [A27]. *Derived in this section:* B6 (exact), the window identity and its inclusion (B7.1, exact), the transfer decomposition and the stem rank bound (B10, exact), the conditional cap (B9.0, exact). *Not derived:* the value of κ — the coherence of M̃, the hard-example count inside the window, and the moment at which w_out crosses 1 − τ are measured, not predicted.

### III-D. Net effect on the layer order: what can be said without a reference draw

**(Rewritten 2026-10-08 after the framework review of 2026-10-07 and the within-stage re-analysis, plan §10.35: the trained-versus-random rank correlation T–R leaves every load-bearing role; the order axis is stated as a within-stage trend that needs no reference.)**

**The statement that needs no reference.** Take the 3×3 convolutions of the third stage (the 14×14 cell) of a residual network. After training their width rises with the block index in every weight we have: 26/26 public weights of six ImageNet families, 22/24 controlled ImageNet runs (A17, A28, A29b, A31), 3/3 CIFAR-10 bottleneck runs [`results/order_within_stage/trend.csv`, plan §10.35]. At γ = 1 initialisation the same cell *falls* with block index (ρ −0.83 to −1.00; the mechanism is the addition step of III-E, Proposition B12), and at γ = 0 initialisation it is flat because every branch is zero [§10.36]. What was called "loss of the initial order" is the difference between these two facts, and it needs neither a reference network nor a cap κ to be stated. The exception is informative: in the CIFAR-10 basic-block network (52 layers, ten classes) the last two stages still fall after training — the few-class collapse of III-C.

**T–R is a property of the pair (profile, draw).** The all-layer T–R of a family changes sign with the draw it is computed against (Table families, columns *draw* and *other draw*: ResNet-50 −0.08 against γ = 1 and +0.44 against γ = 0; ResNet-34 +0.61 / +0.13; ResNet-101 +0.20 / −0.59; ResNet-18 +0.73 either way; A33, §10.22), and so does the within-cell T–R (P9.87–89 fail: the third-stage 3×3 cell is "reversed" against γ = 1 draws in 21/28 ResNet-50 pairs and not against γ = 0 draws, §10.35). An all-layer T–R is moreover carried in part by the alternation of 1×1 and 3×3 levels (DenseNet-121 +0.71, Wide-ResNet-50-2 +0.33, ResNeXt-50 +0.46 fall to −0.26 / −0.17 / −0.22 within the 3×3 role). T–R therefore appears in this paper only as a description with its reference named, never as evidence for "preserved" or "reordered"; no sentence ranks families whose references differ.

**The controlled runs, read as trends and levels.** With the same recipe (SGD, γ = 1 at the start) the third-stage 3×3 width rises after training in ResNet-50 (A17, two seeds), in the 52-layer basic network (A17), in ResNet-18 and ResNet-34 (A29b, two seeds each), and in every ImageNet cell of A28 (100 classes at 224 px; 1,000 classes at 64 px; 100 classes at 64 px, short and long; 130 images per class) and A31 (10 to 100 classes at 224 px, 1,000 images per class) — levels and trends are in plan §10.14, §10.23, §10.29, §10.34. The ten-class A31 cell is degenerate for order statements (15 of 29 layers at k*(0.95) = 1) and is excluded from them; its late-stage widths on held-out images are 0.47–0.89 r_max, so ten classes at 224 px do not reproduce the CIFAR few-class collapse. The attribution language of earlier drafts ("the data at this depth", "depth is the axis", "ten classes do not restore the order") is withdrawn with the statistic it rested on; what the runs show is that the within-stage rise after training is recipe-, depth-, class-count- and resolution-independent within the ranges tried, and that its *initial* counterpart depends on the draw.

**What theory contributes.** No published theorem states a layer order. III-A gives the initial within-stage fall as an exact sampling law per step (B12 at the addition) with no closed form; III-B gives the alignment that precedes the level change; III-C gives the cap in the collapsed few-class case only. The rise along a stage after training has one closed-form link — the ReLU step is the Mehler map of a nearly centred stream, 5/6 blocks within 0.10 [§10.47] — while the trained addition step has not been measured as a law (after training the branches are low-rank and their Jacobians align across blocks — Residual Alignment, Li and Papyan 2023 — so B12's Gaussian-weight hypothesis no longer applies). The order axis is thus a measured regularity with a mechanism at initialisation and a half-mechanism after training; it is not a consequence of a cap, and κ does not enter it.

### III-E. Residual streams: the addition cannot lower the spectrum, only the τ-width

**Known.** For Σ_y = Σ_x + Σ_f + cross terms with ‖cross‖ controlled by ρ_max, λ_i(Σ_y) ≥ λ_{i+r}(Σ_x) (Weyl) and
Σ_y ⪰ (1−ρ)(Σ_x + Σ_f) — classical; the mechanism that residual connections keep rank at initialisation is Dong et al. (2021) for
attention and Joudaki–Hofmann (2024, Prop. 2) for the kernel map; after training the branches are low-rank (Li and Papyan 2023).

**Proposition B2 (τ-width at the addition) — closed 2026-10-01.** Its cross-term (Ky Fan) form is valid in all 80 measured blocks but reaches a median 0.24 of the sum's τ = 0.95 width in trained ResNets (4/56 blocks above one half; plan §10.17); it stays out of the paper. What can be said without it: the absolute spectrum cannot fall (Weyl), the relative width can, and in ResNets it does (below).

**What we verify [§10.17].** In 56 blocks of four ImageNet ResNets every eigenvalue of the stream at its own width is *larger* after the
addition (retention ≥ 1.11, median 1.36–5.77), both lower bounds hold in every block, and yet the τ = 0.95 width before the ReLU is
narrower than the stream's in 36 of 42 non-downsampling blocks — the gap between an absolute and a relative statement. The block output after
the ReLU exceeds the conv3 upper bound r_max in 161/161 bottlenecks [round 2], so the r_max bound is a statement about convolution
outputs, not about the stream.

**Within a stage [§10.36–10.47; theory bet, day one, 2026-10-07].** ResNet-50 at γ = 1 initialisation, layer3 (C = 1024), with x the stream entering a block, f the branch, y = x + f and z = ReLU(y) the next block's stream, all at τ = 0.95. The stream narrows along the stage (0.082 → 0.060 of C from block 1 to 5), and the narrowing sits in the addition, not in the ReLU: y is narrower than x in 4/6 blocks and |z − y| ≤ 2 channels in every block [§10.37]. The branch is far narrower than the stream (43–60 against 52–93 channels; conv3 is a 1×1 expansion with r_max = C/4) and carries about twice its energy, so the sum concentrates. Three registered mechanisms for the concentration all fail: successive branches' top subspaces overlap at the chance level k/C (0.04–0.06) [§10.43]; branch and stream of the same block overlap at chance [§10.44]; the cross term Σ_xf along the stream's top subspace is 1–9 % of the stream's energy there [§10.45]. A random-rotation superposition of the two measured spectra reproduces the fall (104 → 81) and over-predicts the width by about 20 % in every block [§10.44]: the gap does not shrink with the channel count (0.06–0.35 in all 56 blocks of four random ResNets, growing with stage depth in ResNet-34/50) and sits in the stream–branch dependence: the cross term Σ_xf has trace ≈ 0 and is 1–9 % along the stream's top directions, but along the *sum's* top-k directions it carries 12–26 % of the total energy [§10.48, P9.105–107]. The addition step is therefore not a free additive convolution of the two spectra even in the limit. What it is, exactly: with h the input of the block's last 1×1 convolution and J = Cov([x; h]), y = x + W₃h and Σ_y = [I W₃] J [I W₃]ᵀ, whose law is a function of J and the scale of W₃ alone when W₃ is i.i.d. Gaussian — the layer-local law of S6 with one cross block. Sampling W₃ given the measured J reproduces the measured width within 5 % in 13/13 blocks of layer2–4 (draw-to-draw spread 0.5–2.2 channels); the same draws with the cross block set to zero are off by 11–51 %, growing with depth as the cross block's relative energy grows (0.23 → 0.40) [§10.49, P9.108]. The fall is a property of the architecture alone — the A7 surrogate reproduces it on white noise (ρ = −0.94) [§10.40] — and a stream that enters narrower than its branches rises instead (CIFAR images at 224 px; ResNet-18, whose basic branch at 128 channels is wider than the stream) [§10.38, §10.40].

The ReLU step has a known closed form for jointly Gaussian input (Mehler; Daniely et al. 2016): Σ_z = D[Σ_k (a_k²/k!) R^{∘k}]D with D = diag(σ) and R the correlation matrix. At initialisation it fails: it over-predicts the ReLU widening by 20–28 % because the stream is non-centred (|mean|/std 1.2–1.7, a sum of non-negative post-ReLU outputs), while the measured ReLU step is ≈ 0 in bottleneck blocks and −10 to −20 % in basic blocks. After training the stream is nearly centred (0.2–0.5) and the closed form predicts the ReLU widening within 0.10 in 5/6 blocks (+0.64/+0.39/+0.29/+0.25/+0.23 against the measured +0.50/+0.30/+0.23/+0.23/+0.26) [§10.47]. After training the addition narrows and the ReLU widens, and the stream rises along stage 3 in 9/9 checkpoints (0.44 → 0.82) [§10.36]; the trained chain therefore has one closed-form link.

Exact statements of ours here: Lemma B11.0 (the τ-width is subadditive, a consequence of Ky Fan's maximum principle — check Bhatia's Ky Fan norms before submission) and Proposition B11.2 (the stream's width is bounded by the sum of the τ′-widths of the last J branches), valid in the 5/5 usable blocks and loose by 4–6× [§10.42]; both stay in the proofs note. What this paragraph claims: the within-stage trend at initialisation is the numerical result of a chain of known maps — layer-local law for the branch, free additive convolution at the addition, width-preserving ReLU — — now each a conditional sampling law that is exact given the previous link (S6 for every convolution; the joint law [§10.49] at the addition; the ReLU measured at ≤ 2 channels) — with no closed form for the width at any link; after training the ReLU link has a closed form that hits. No theorem of ours.

## What this section does and does not claim (for the framework review)

- Organisation, not a claim: the single principle (Ky Fan) and the single chain (forward at initialisation, backward after training) of the preamble are a way of stating known pieces, and the three-widths distinction (PCA-optimal / used / needed) is a reading of D5, A25, A34 and P-b7.2, not a theorem. Citations the preamble adds: Cui et al. 2020 for the gradient-side count, Grosse and Martens 2016 for Σ_patch and Γ as the K-FAC factors, Hsu et al. 2022 for variance ≠ task importance (`gradient-weighted-width-prior-art-2026-10-07.md`).
- Claims (2026-10-11): the theorem-form summary at the head of III-A assembles known pieces; the exact statements of ours in it are Lemma M.2.0 with Theorem M.2 (Lipschitz concentration carried through the ReLU and the centring; elementary), T1.3, T2.2′, T2.3, T2a.1–2, and the measured gap per link.
- Claims: each link is a published theorem or a measured fact; the assembly (III-D) is ours; the exact statements of ours are small — B6 (no stationary point in conv–BN layers), B7.1 (the window identity for SGD with momentum, sharpening a known EMA view), B10 (the transfer decomposition Σ_out = Γ^{1/2} M̃ Γ^{1/2} and the stem rank bound), B9.0 (the conditional cap P·K − 1); B2 is withdrawn. The measured *degree of hypothesis failure* per link, and the location of the narrowing in the coherence of M̃, are the parts a reviewer cannot find elsewhere.
- Does not claim: a theorem for the within-stage width of the residual stream — the three targets of the 2026-10-07 bet ((a) surrogate, (b) sufficient condition at the addition, (c) ReLU closed form) each closed as a known map plus its measured range (III-E); a derivation of κ — the coherence of M̃ is measured, not predicted; a finite-width, correlated-position law for the ReLU step (open; B4); that the layer order is explained (III-D states a within-stage regularity with a mechanism at initialisation only; T–R carries no claim).
- Weakest points: III-A(iii) sample independence; III-C: the window inclusion is loose by an order of magnitude on full-width networks (an ordering, not a bound), the late level is nearly flat in λ above 5e-4, B9's premise holds in one setting only (32 px) and fails at 48 px, and the canonical-representation hypothesis gives neither basis nor exponent; III-D: the order axis is a trend statement without a theorem; the trained addition step is unmeasured as a law.
